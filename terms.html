04

The master password is yours alone

VaultKey derives its encryption keys from the master password you choose at setup. It does not store that password, does not send it anywhere, and holds no copy that could be produced later. This is the whole point of the design — and its whole risk:

  • There is no reset flow. No email link, no recovery code, no support ticket, no back door.
  • Forgetting it makes the vault, and any cloud copy of it, permanently unreadable. Support cannot help; neither can Google; neither can anyone who obtains the file.
  • Choosing a password you cannot keep — because it is too weak to survive guessing, or too unusual to survive a week — is a decision only you can make badly.

Keep one offline record of it that is not inside the vault. A piece of paper in a drawer is a better recovery plan than any feature this app could add.

06

Sideloading and the signing key

VaultKey is distributed as an APK from this site, not through Google Play. Two consequences need stating plainly rather than hidden in a footnote:

  • You install it yourself. Android will warn you about an unknown source. That warning is correct: nothing between this site and your phone proves the file was not substituted on the way, and there is no store review process behind it.
  • The APK is signed with the Android debug certificate — the standard development key, not a private publication key. It is honest about what the build is, and it means another app signed with the same debug key could, on a technical level, look like an update to this one. Verify the download over HTTPS from this site and check the SHA-256 shown in the release notes if that matters to you.

Verify what you installed before putting anything valuable in it. That is a fair price for a free, self-published app.

Your Google account and Drive

Optional sync stores one encrypted file in your own Google Drive application-data folder. It is your storage, your account, and Google's terms apply to it alongside these. You are responsible for the account you sign in with, for keeping its recovery options working, and for the storage quota.

Sync can stop without warning for reasons outside this app's control: a consent screen that lapses, a token that Google refuses to renew, an app moved out of verification, a Drive API change, or a Google account suspension. When that happens the local vault remains intact and readable — which is why the app is designed local-first rather than cloud-with-a-cache.

You own everything in the vault, and you also carry the risk of losing it. Back up: export an encrypted backup file from the Backup screen and keep it somewhere the phone is not the only copy of. Test the restore at least once — an untested backup is a story you tell yourself.

You are responsible for the lawfulness of what you store and for keeping your devices updated, patched, and physically yours. Encryption protects stored rows; it does nothing about a phone left unlocked on a table.

Updates and availability

New builds appear on this site when they are ready and not a moment earlier. There is no update schedule, no compatibility promise across versions, and no obligation to keep any version downloadable. A future release may change formats; where a change could lose data, the app will refuse rather than guess — but you should still expect that an old APK stops being offered.

The app can tell you when a newer build exists, if you ask it to: Check for updates on the About screen fetches one small manifest from this site and compares its build number with the one installed. It downloads nothing until you tap the same row again, verifies the SHA-256 the manifest declares before handing the file over, and cannot install anything on its own — Android's own sheet stays the last word. A manifest whose URL, hash or size does not parse is discarded and the row says so.

This site, and the APK hosted on it, may be taken down at any time. That ends distribution; it does not unlock your vault or hand anything to a third party.
