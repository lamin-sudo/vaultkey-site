03 / faq

The questions that come up.

I forgot my master password. Can you reset it?

No, and no one else can either. The keys that decrypt the vault are derived from that password with Argon2id, and the app never stores it — there is no recovery path to switch on, by design rather than by omission.

If you have an encrypted backup made earlier from the Backup screen, you can restore it into a new vault. Otherwise the vault and any cloud copy of it are permanently unreadable. Clearing the app's data starts you again with an empty vault.

Is my password visible to Google when sync is on?

No. Before anything is uploaded, records are re-encrypted with a separate random sync key that belongs to the vault, and your master password is only used to wrap that key. Google stores the file, and sees a name, a size and an opaque payload.

Sync uses one permission only — drive.appdata — which is a hidden folder reserved for the app's own data. It cannot read the rest of your Drive.

Why does the vault lock itself so quickly?

Because an unlocked vault in a pocket is a readable one. Backgrounding the app ends the session and zeroes the data key; the timeout is yours to set in Settings → Auto-lock (from 30 seconds up to never).

After five wrong attempts the vault locks out, and the wait grows with each failure: 30 seconds, then 5 minutes, then 30 minutes. Support cannot shorten it and clearing it would defeat it — waiting it out is the intended path, and your data is untouched while you wait.

Autofill offers nothing in apps and browsers.

Autofill is a system role, not a permission the app can grant itself. Go to Settings → Autofill inside VaultKey — it opens the Android autofill picker — and choose VaultKey as the autofill service. On some Android builds you also have to pick it under System settings → Passwords & accounts → Autofill service.

Then unlock the vault: an autofill request against a locked vault cannot produce a credential, and the app will ask you to unlock rather than invent one.

Why can't I screenshot the app, or record the screen?

The app sets FLAG_SECURE on its window, so Android refuses capture and hides the vault from the Recents thumbnail. This is deliberate protection against a password appearing in a screenshot you later shared somewhere, and there is no switch to turn it off.

How do I move my vault to a new phone?

Two supported routes, and using both is the cautious answer:

Sync: sign in with the same Google account on the new device, tap Sync now, and enter the master password when asked once so the new device can unwrap the existing sync key.

Backup file: export from Backup on the old phone, copy the file across, and restore it on the new one — including into a brand-new vault.

Can I bring passwords in from my browser?

Yes — Settings → Import reads exports from Chrome, Firefox, or a CSV file, and lets you choose what to do with rows that already exist. Export from the browser first (each one hides it under a different “show passwords / export” menu), then pick the file.

Which Android versions are supported?

Android 8.0 (API 26) and newer; the app targets Android 15. Biometric unlock needs a working enrolled fingerprint or face profile, and passkey support depends on the system credential provider being available on your build.

Is there a web version, an iPhone app, or a browser extension?

No. VaultKey is Android only, and the vault format is not built to be read by anything else. If you need iPhone or a browser extension, plan on a manager that ships them — storing your only copy of a vault on a platform you cannot reach is a bad trade.
